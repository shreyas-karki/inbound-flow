1 · The journey

From ad click to next step, in two lanes

When the lead hits submit, two things start at once. Their browser goes to our page and waits for a decision. Unbounce sends the same form to Make. Both ask the app for the decision, and the app scores each lead once and shares the answer for 2 hours, so the calendar the lead sees always matches the level in Slack.

Level 3 floor: a district under $30k Total PARR, or with a Starbridge score under 50, never gets a First Touch. Those leads become Level 2 Discovery with the BDR, whoever asks. Without PARR, under 650 students counts instead. With no data the floor doesn't apply.

Also: a message that reveals a student, parent, vendor or high-school-only teacher drops to Level 0 whatever the role. A Starbridge "Blocked" district, or one that just picked another program, drops one level. Decision makers who answer "I'm not sure" at a Strong or Ideal fit district are Level 3. Principals count as decision makers in NYC, charters, privates and single-school districts; otherwise they're champions.

4 · Bookings

Getting booked meetings into Salesforce

Booking in Salesforce shows up on Google Calendar through Einstein Activity Capture. The other direction doesn't: when a lead books on a growth manager's Google appointment page, nothing reaches Salesforce.

Step 1

Lead picks a time

On the host's Google appointment page, embedded on our thank-you page.

Step 2

Google Calendar event

On the host's calendar, with the lead's email as a guest. The lead gets the invite.

Step 3 · missing

Salesforce Event

Never created. The booking only exists if the host logs it by hand.

Step 4

Reporting

First-touch counts, booking rate per landing page, 48-hour no-book follow-ups.

This blocks the A/B test's main number. Goal 2 is "more bookings" (today about 1 in 5 decision makers book). If test-arm bookings only exist in Google Calendar, the per-page first-touch report can't count them.

Google Calendar does have an API. It can list events, search them by guest email, and send a notification when a calendar changes, and Make has Google Calendar modules built on it. The real limitation is narrower: Google appointment pages have no "someone just booked" webhook like Calendly does, so we have to watch the calendars and spot new events whose guest is a lead we just routed.

Option A · check first

Turn on Google → Salesforce sync in Einstein

Einstein Activity Capture has two separate features. Activity capture shows Google events on the activity timeline but doesn't create real Event records, so they can't be reported on. Event sync creates Event records, and its direction can be "Salesforce to Google" only, or both ways.

  • Ask RevOps which one the growth managers' Einstein configuration uses, and in which direction. "Salesforce to Google only" matches what you're seeing exactly.
  • No build. Check that synced events land on the right Contact and Account, and whether they can carry a type like First Touch.
Option B · fallback

Make watches the hosts' calendars

Already on the plan as the "bookings tracker".

  • Google Calendar "Watch events" on each host's calendar.
  • Match the guest email to a recent inbound lead.
  • Create the Salesforce Event (Contact, Account, First Touch or Discovery) and post "Booked" in Slack.
  • Each host connects Google to Make once, or shares their calendar with one account.
Option C · most robust

The app reads calendars directly

  • A Google Workspace service account that can read the hosts' calendars (needs a Google admin to approve).
  • Google pushes a notification on every change; the app matches the guest to the lead it routed and writes the Event.
  • Also gives us the booked or not-booked answer for the email branch later.

Recommendation: ask RevOps about option A today. If synced events don't land on the Contact, build option B before launch, so test-arm bookings are counted from day one. Either way, booking detection also unlocks the things we parked: a different email for booked vs not booked, the 48-hour no-book follow-up, and reminder emails for no-shows.
